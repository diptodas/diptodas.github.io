## Course Overview

Computers and Society introduces the many interconnections between computing and society. Students examine the values that drive today's computer industry and how those values align with, or depart from, the moral values held by different communities. Drawing on theories from philosophy and the social sciences, the course builds a deep understanding of the ethical tensions in the relationship between computers and society, while strengthening students' writing, debating, and design skills so they can become ethical, responsible, and caring computer scientists.

The course is organized into four modules: basic concepts, data and society, computing, fairness, and equity, and computing, labor, and materiality.

## Learning Objectives

By the end of this course, students should be able to:

- Define and explain key concepts in the course, such as ethics, privacy, and equity
- Develop a critical perspective on the recent evolution of computing and software technology and its impact on society
- Develop a deeper understanding of the ethical tensions around emerging computing practices
- Think critically about, and develop methods for, ethical applications of computing technologies
- Develop the writing skills needed to articulate an argument in a scholarly discussion

## Assessment

- **Reading responses (60%):** four written responses, one after each module
- **Tutorial participation (18%):** activities in weekly TA-led tutorials that build on the lectures
- **In-term assessment (22%):** a two-hour in-person exam with multiple-choice and short-answer questions
