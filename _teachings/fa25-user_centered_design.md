## Course Overview

This course surveys the theoretical and practical foundations of human-computer interaction and user-centered design. Students learn theories of interaction, including cognitive, organizational, collaborative, and task-based approaches, along with user interface design techniques, design guidelines, and usability testing. The course explores a range of interfaces (desktop, mobile, touch, vision, audio) and contexts (personal, organizational, cross-cultural), and asks students to consider how designs are political and embedded with power.

## Learning Objectives

After completing this course, students will be able to:

- Analytically interpret the interplay between people, tasks, technologies, and contexts
- Gather and understand user requirements
- Apply user-centered design and HCI principles, methods, techniques, and guidelines to technology design and development
- Conduct evaluations and user studies

## Course Project

In teams of four to five, students carry a project of their choice through the full user-centered design cycle, with milestones for a proposal, user requirements, prototypes, and evaluation. The project culminates in a final paper of about 4,000 words in the ACM format and a showcase presentation in place of a final exam.

## Assessment

- **Participation (10%):** engagement in discussions, peer feedback, and team collaboration
- **Reading reflections (40%):** four reflections on the module readings
- **Course project (50%):** proposal (5%), user requirements (10%), prototypes (10%), evaluation (10%), and final report (15%)
