> **Research Summary**
>
> This research moves beyond the traditional focus on host-country integration to explore how Global South migrants and diasporas exercise political, economic, and cultural agency across borders through digital and financial infrastructures. It also examines how immigrant-led organizations are excluded from digital and AI ecosystems, and designs technology that supports them.

---

## Why this research?

Migration does not end people's participation in their homelands, nor does arrival guarantee inclusion in the technologies of their new home. Diasporas mobilize online and through remittance flows to shape politics thousands of miles away, while migrants navigate religious, cultural, and communal disruptions, and immigrant nonprofits struggle to be recognized by the platforms and AI tools their work increasingly depends on.

Questions guiding this work include:

- How do diasporas use platforms and financial infrastructures to participate in homeland politics?
- How do migrants sustain religious and cultural practice through technology after migration?
- How are immigrant-led organizations excluded from digital and AI ecosystems?
- How can AI support, rather than displace, the cooperative work of marginalized immigrant groups?

---

## Research Approach

- Semi-structured interviews and trace ethnography with digital diasporas, such as the non-resident Bangladeshi community
- A systems approach to "residual mobilities": the socio-cultural, psychological, and spiritual transformations that follow migration
- Co-design and evaluation of inclusive technologies with immigrant organizations

## Research Outcomes and Contributions

- **Diasporic superposition:** During Bangladesh's 2024 pro-democracy movement, non-resident Bangladeshis built transnational networks, organized a remittance boycott that reframed economic dependence as political leverage, and adapted to government surveillance and information blackouts. We theorize how diasporas exercise influence from hybrid positions of privilege and vulnerability. (See [CHI 26](/assets/pdf/CHI26_remittance.pdf))

- **Residual mobilities and religious practice:** Hindu migrants in Canada use social media and videoconferencing to organize festivals, stay connected to their religious community, and pass traditions to the next generation while navigating religious diversity at work. (See [CSCW 25](/assets/pdf/CSCW25_migrants.pdf))

- **Infrastructural immobility:** Iranian immigrant nonprofits face legitimacy barriers, capacity gaps, and ethical dilemmas that exclude them from the digital nonprofit ecosystem, and uncritical adoption of generative AI risks deepening that marginalization. (See [DIS 26](/assets/pdf/DIS26_nonprofit.pdf))

- **AMINA:** Building on these findings, we co-designed and evaluated AMINA, an AI assistant that supports immigrant nonprofits' everyday operations, recognizes informal nonprofit work, counters politically charged misinformation, and supports multilingual interaction. (See [GROUP 27](/assets/pdf/GROUP27_AMINA.pdf))

- **Resources for studying transnational discourse:** BTPD, a hand-curated multilingual dataset of Bengali transnational political discourse across online communities. (See [CSCW 25 Poster](/assets/pdf/CSCW25_BTPD.pdf))
